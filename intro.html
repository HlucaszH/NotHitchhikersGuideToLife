Introduction

The part every book has at the front that nobody reads. Read this one.

A note before we start

From the guy writing this at midnight instead of sleeping

Okay so. Here's the deal. This isn't a textbook, it's not a course some company sells for 400 zł with a certificate at the end that means nothing. It's me, writing down the stuff that actually helped me get from "understands memes" to "can hold a real conversation," in the order I think will work for you specifically. Or at least for me that worked/s for me.

I don't know if this will work for you. I don't know if it will work for anyone. But I do know that if you read this, and do the assignments, and actually try to use English in real life, you'll get better at English. And you'll get better at life. That's all I care about, since this is why I'm writing this.

A few ground rules, because every good introduction has ground rules:

1. I will be annoying about this

Expect jokes that don't land, tangents, and the occasional "just trust me" instead of a perfect explanation. That's on purpose — you're more likely to remember a stupid joke than a grammar rule stated flatly. Sometimes what works is mystically obvious, sometimes it's not.

2. Homework isn't optional, sorry

You don't get better at English by just reading about English. You get better by embarrassing yourself in English, on paper, where I can fix it before you do it out loud somewhere it matters. You get better by doing the homework. You get better by asking dummy questions and learning from your mistakes. You get better when you say something that doesn't make sense or is so wrong I WILL laugh at it, BUT I will help you fix it. That's the point. That is how we adapt and overcome. That is how we learn.

3. Ask when you don't get it

I have to write it in a different point on its own so you will remember it. If something doesn't make sense, say so. I'd rather explain it a second, dumber way than have you nod along and stay confused. You will learn faster that way. I promise.
