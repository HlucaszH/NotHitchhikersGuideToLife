Present Perfect vs. Past Simple

The tense fight that has ruined more Polish-to-English conversations than anything else on this planet. Sit down, this one's long.

Why we're even doing this

A short rant before the actual grammar

Okay so. In Polish you've got one past tense and it does everything. "Zrobiłem to" covers "I did it," "I have done it," and honestly half the time "I've been doing it" too, depending on context and how tired you are. English looked at that beautifully simple system and said "no, we're going to need at least two tenses for that, and they're going to sound almost the same, and we're going to make you guess which one based on vibes."

That's not entirely fair to English, there is an actual rule, and once it clicks it clicks for good. But until it clicks, every single sentence about the past is a small gamble. So let's fix that.

The one-sentence version, if you read nothing else: Past Simple is for a finished thing at a known time. Present Perfect is for a thing where the time doesn't matter, or where the result still matters right now. Everything below is just that idea, said five more times in five more ways until it sticks.

1. The two forms, side by side

Structure first, meaning later — you need to be able to build the sentence before you can decide which one to build

Tense Structure Example
Past Simple subject + verbpast (regular: -ed, irregular: learn the list, sorry) I watched that movie. / She went home.
Present Perfect subject + have/has + verbpast participle I have watched that movie. / She has gone home.

Quick reminders, because I know somebody needs them:

  • Regular verbs get -ed in both past simple and past participle: work → worked → worked. Boring, reliable, no drama.
  • Irregular verbs have their own past simple AND their own past participle, and they're often different from each other: go → went → gone, see → saw → seen, write → wrote → written. There is no trick here. You just have to know them. I'm not sorry, English isn't sorry, nobody's sorry.
  • Negatives and questions: Past Simple uses did/didn't ("Did you go?" / "I didn't go"), Present Perfect uses have/haven't or has/hasn't ("Have you gone?" / "I haven't gone").

2. So when do I actually use which one

This is the part people skip and then get everything wrong forever, so don't skip it

Use Past Simple when the action happened at a specific, known point in the past — even if you don't say the exact time out loud, both people basically know when it was, and it's over, done, finished, in a box, in the past.

Use Present Perfect when either: (a) you don't care about, or don't know, exactly when it happened — you just care that it happened at some point in your life up to now — or (b) the action is finished but its result is still relevant right now, in this exact conversation.

Situation Tense Why
Talking about a specific day, date, or "back then" moment Past Simple Time is known and the story is closed
Talking about life experience, no specific time in mind Present Perfect You're summarizing your whole life up to now, not one moment
The action just happened and it's still affecting the present Present Perfect The result matters more than the moment it happened
Telling a story with a clear timeline (first this, then that) Past Simple You're narrating events in order — that's Past Simple's whole job

3. Signal words — your cheat sheet

Half the time you can guess the right tense just from one word in the sentence. Use this shamelessly.

Goes with Past Simple Goes with Present Perfect
yesterday, last week/month/year already
in 2019, on Monday, at 5pm yet (in questions/negatives)
ago (two days ago, a while ago) just
when I was a kid ever / never
that day, back then since (a point in time: since 2020)
  for (a duration: for three years)

If you see "ago" or a specific date, your brain should scream Past Simple. If you see "ever", "never", "already", or "yet", it should scream Present Perfect. This alone will save you more often than actually understanding the grammar. I said what I said.

4. The mistakes I already know you're going to make

Because I made every single one of these too

Wrong Right Why it's wrong
I have seen him yesterday. I saw him yesterday. "Yesterday" is a specific time. The second you name a specific time, Present Perfect isn't allowed in the room anymore.
Did you ever eat sushi? Have you ever eaten sushi? "Ever" about your whole life = Present Perfect, not Past Simple.
I have lost my keys yesterday, so I couldn't get in. I lost my keys yesterday, so I couldn't get in. Tempting because "the result still matters," but the specific time word "yesterday" overrules that. Time word wins.
I live here since three years. I have lived here for three years. / I have lived here since 2023. An action that started in the past and is still true now needs Present Perfect, not Present Simple. Also: "for" + duration, "since" + starting point — don't mix them up.

5. The 10-second gut check

When you genuinely don't know which one, ask yourself this

  • Can I attach a specific time to this sentence and it still sounds normal ("...yesterday", "...in 2021", "...when I was 12")? → Past Simple.
  • Am I talking about my life in general, with no specific moment in mind? → Present Perfect.
  • Did this just happen and do I care about the effect right now, not the moment itself? → Present Perfect.
  • Am I telling a story, moving from one event to the next? → Past Simple, basically always, for the whole story.
